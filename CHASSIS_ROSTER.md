Twelve proposed factory builds for the control-focused rebuild. Mass is ready-to-drive mass including the powertrain and fluids. It is a target for the assembled vehicle, separate from the bare chassis mass in the current catalog. Power, torque and dimensions are design targets. Final values depend on the rebuilt handling model and playtests.

## Factory personalities and meaningful upgrades

### Apex GT-86R

The approachable starter. A low hood, clear sight lines and progressive rear slip give new players a chance to catch a mistake. The factory engine leaves room to feel every gear. It gives up straight-line pull to heavier turbo cars. Start with street tires and a mild limited-slip diff. Offer a short final drive as the first trade-off, with stronger exits and earlier upshifts.

### Pippin 660

A tiny rear-engine city car with upright glass, skinny tires and a very short nose. It threads tight gates and lets players work near the limit at neighborhood speeds. The short wheelbase and rear weight make abrupt lift-off a handful. Offer street tires and a front ballast or rear cooling-duct option. Supports small garages and low-speed circuits where adding huge power yields little benefit.

### Mica S

A square three-door grocery hatch with compliant suspension and strong forward visibility. It is an inexpensive platform for learning front grip and putting power down out of a wet bend. Too much throttle while turning asks the front tires to do both jobs. Use narrow street tires and an open diff from the factory. First upgrades are a helical diff and a brake pad package.

### Dovetail 2.5

A long, angular executive sedan whose weight and wheelbase give the player time to hold a slide. The straight-six lets throttle changes come in progressively. Transitions take commitment and a long run heats the rear tires. Fit street tires and a mild LSD by default. A drift differential, more steering angle and an oil cooler make a convincing specialist build with costs in tire wear and everyday manners.

### Lantern MPV

A tall minivan with a big windshield, upright seating and a visibly rolling body. It gives beginners good road visibility, a progressive front-drive breakaway and forgiving suspension. Quick left-right changes and repeated heavy stops expose the mass. Keep the factory ride comfortable. Let players remove rear seats, change anti-roll bars and fit a better cooling pack, with handling and mass changes the player can see.

### Quarry 240

A single-cab work pickup with a broad nose, long bed and a torquey engine. It tracks well on broken surfaces and carries momentum in contact events. An empty bed leaves the rear axle light, so throttle and braking require care. Offer bed ballast with adjustable placement, an optional locking differential and an underbody protection package. Ballast helps traction and hurts braking. Keep stock speed modest through gearing and drag.

### Serrac Touring

A boxy AWD wagon with long roof rails, useful in cool rain and on mixed grip. It pulls cleanly out of slow corners and gives drivers confidence under power. Entry understeer and driveline mass make it less nimble on a dry tight course. Stock with all-season street tires. Let players tune torque split and choose whether to work on wet tires or cooling first.

### Stratos Wedge EVO

A compact mid-engine wedge with a low eye point and quick rotation. It puts power down well when the driver is smooth. Abrupt lifts and mid-corner corrections are less forgiving than in the Apex. Offer a calmer factory setup on street tires. Toe, diff locking and cooling airflow become the deep-tuning choices when a driver wants faster direction changes.

### Juniper Club

A tiny open roadster with narrow fenders and very little mass. It makes carrying speed and choosing a clean line feel rewarding with modest horsepower. It loses on long straights and gives up stability to longer cars in fast sweeps. Use light wheels and ordinary street rubber. Offer a final-drive swap and a choice between compliant suspension and a firmer slalom setup.

### Broadside 440

A broad-shouldered older V8 coupe with slow body roll, obvious weight transfer and enough torque to sustain a long arc. It feels at home on sweeping drift sections. Tight transitions, stopping distance and tire heat all work against it. Offer a modern brake package and shorter gearing. Stronger brakes help entry control while added speed still has to be managed.

### Relay Parcel

A short electric delivery van with a high seat and a low battery floor. Instant motor response and strong short-range acceleration make it interesting on stop-and-go courses. Battery mass and thermal limits make longer high-load runs a different proposition. Give it adjustable regenerative-braking strength and battery cooling in Advanced. This vehicle needs a distinct powertrain module and can follow the combustion chassis.

### Valkyrie LMP-X

A closed-cockpit prototype with a wide stance, short sight lines and effective aero on smooth tracks. It rewards precise inputs and earns its pace once the aero wakes up. Slow turns, bumps and splitter damage expose its compromises. Offer an endurance cooling package and adjustable aero balance. Use class-based events so the prototype, the pickup and the minivan each have a reason to be chosen.

## Build order and guardrails

1. **Baseline and connectivity first.** Use the [rebuild plan](MODULUS_REBUILD_PLAN.md) for the direct-only friend lobby and the first playable driving slice.
2. **Apex, Dovetail, Quarry.** The first three assets cover a forgiving starter, a drift car and a heavy contact vehicle. Build their cockpit, silhouette, collision hull and damage feedback far enough that each is recognizable from the driver’s seat.
3. **Pippin, Mica, Lantern, Serrac.** Add distinct low-speed, wet and utility personalities after the common chassis pipeline works.
4. **Stratos, Juniper, Broadside, Valkyrie, Relay.** Rebuild existing performance cars and add the more specialized vehicles around the events that give them a purpose.

Use a body, a cockpit and a simple collision hull per chassis. Build the factory drivetrain, tires, cooling, gearing and mass distribution from data. Add chassis-specific hardpoints, steering lock, driver-eye position, gearing and brake capacity before treating a vehicle as a finished model. Keep the chassis IDs for existing saves and give the new balance a separate version.

Prove the differences at modest speeds. In one technical sector the Apex should recover cleanly, the Dovetail should hold a predictable slide and the Quarry should make mass felt at brake-in. Each should have situations that reward it and situations that expose its trade-offs.
